Hand Anglage & Inward Angles: The Pinnacle of Haute Horlogerie Finishing

By WristMechanic Movement Research Division • 181 Mercer St Master Horology Unit • 14 Min Deep Read
The pinnacle of haute horlogerie finishing: hand anglage, berceau rounded bevels, and razor-sharp inward mirror angles (angles rentrants).

1. The Philosophical & Functional Purpose of Hand Anglage

In high-end mechanical watchmaking, a caliber's bridges and plates begin as raw machined plates with sharp, burred, 90-degree edges. If left unfinished, microscopic metallic burrs can detach and jam the gear train pinions.

Historically, watchmakers chamfered these edges to remove burrs. Over centuries, this functional step evolved into the highest artistic expression of haute horlogerie: Anglage (hand-beveling). Hand anglage transforms flat bridge perimeters into gleaming, mirror-polished, light-refracting curves that define the boundary between industrial manufacturing and museum-grade master artistry.

2. Berceau (Rounded Convex) vs Flat 45° Chamfering Geometry

There are two primary styles of anglage in modern horology:

  • Flat Anglage (Chanfrein Plat): The edge is beveled at a crisp, flat 45-degree angle. While demanding, flat anglage can be partially replicated by CNC diamond milling tools.
  • Rounded Anglage (Anglage en Berceau): The pinnacle of Swiss and Saxon watchmaking (practiced by masters like Philippe Dufour and A. Lange & Söhne). The artisan files the bridge edge into a gentle, continuous convex cylindrical curve that slopes seamlessly from the top bridge surface to the vertical flank. The berceau curve captures and rolls ambient light dynamically as the watch moves, creating an unmistakable liquid gleam.

3. The Hand Tool Suite: Degussit Stones, Needle Files & Micro-Scrapers

Executing authentic hand anglage requires an array of specialized hand tools:

1. Swiss Steel Needle Files: Precision cut #4 and #6 files used to establish the initial 45-degree chamfer geometry and remove CNC tooling marks.
2. Degussit Ceramic Stones: Fine-grained red ceramic stones (sintered aluminum oxide) lubricated with light oil to smooth file ridges.
3. Hardened Steel Micro-Scrapers (Ébarboirs): Razor-sharp hand tools used to shave away microscopic metal layers in delicate corners.

4. Polishing with Gentian Wood & Diamond Abrasive Suspensions

To achieve a flawless, distortion-free optical mirror polish (glace), synthetic rubber wheels are useless because they round off crisp edges.

Instead, master artisans use dried stems of the Wild Gentian Plant (Gentiana lutea) gathered from the Swiss Jura mountains. Gentian wood possesses an ideal balance of fibrous softness and dense structural hold. The watchmaker shapes the gentian pegwood into a fine chisel point, coats it with progressive diamond pastes (starting at 3 microns and finishing with 0.5-micron diamond paste), and rubs the beveled edge by hand for hours until all micro-scratches vanish.

5. The Ultimate Benchmark: Razor Inward Angles (Angles Rentrants)

The definitive proof of hand craftsmanship is the Sharp Inward Angle (angle rentrant)—where two chamfers meet inside an internal V-shaped corner of a bridge.

Automated CNC milling cutters and diamond wheels are rotary tools; because a rotary tool has a physical radius (typically 0.1mm or larger), it is physically impossible for a machine to cut a sharp, zero-radius internal corner. A machine always leaves a rounded curve. Creating a razor-sharp internal corner requires the artisan to file the intersection by hand with a triangular needle file, carving a clean, seamless knife-edge line that splits ambient light down to the sub-micron vertex.

6. Rounded Outward Corners (Angles Sortis) & Corner Radii Symmetry

Equally demanding are rounded outward corners (angles sortis). As the bevel rounds an external corner, the width of the chamfer must remain mathematically constant without widening, narrowing, or losing its crisp boundary lines.

7. Poinçon de Genève & Fleurier Quality Foundation Requirements

Prestigious quality certifications mandate strict hand anglage standards:

  • Poinçon de Genève (Geneva Seal): Law mandates that all bridges must have polished chamfers, sinkings must be polished, and all functional edges must be deburred with zero visible machining marks under magnification.
  • Qualité Fleurier: Requires 100% aesthetic finishing across all components, including hidden underside surfaces.

8. Finishing Maillechort (German Silver) vs Grade 5 Titanium Bridges

Different metals pose unique challenges during hand anglage:

  • Maillechort (German Silver): Softer than steel, requiring a delicate touch to avoid over-filing, but yielding a rich, buttery, warm luster when polished with gentian wood.
  • Grade 5 Titanium: Extremely tough and gummy under the file, causing rapid tool wear. Titanium requires specialized diamond-impregnated ceramic files to achieve mirror-polished bevels.

9. 20x Loupe & Optical Microscopy Quality Inspection Protocols

Under 20x optical magnification, hand anglage reveals its perfection:

The mirror-polished chamfer must exhibit zero longitudinal striations, zero orange-peel ripples, and sharp, perfectly straight boundary lines where the bevel meets the top Geneva stripes and the vertical brushed flanks.

10. Preserving the Endangered Soul of Traditional Hand Watchmaking

A single haute horlogerie movement can require over 40 hours of pure hand anglage labor by a single master finisher. In an era of automated industrial mass production, hand anglage remains the ultimate testament to human dedication, patience, and artistic excellence.

11. Mirror Black Polishing (Poli Noir) on Hardened Steel Screws

In haute horlogerie movements, every screw head is finished with Black Polish (poli noir or spéculaire). The screw is clamped in a tripod tool and rubbed in figure-eight motions across a flat zinc plate coated with 0.5-micron diamond powder. When viewed directly from above, the perfectly flat, distortion-free surface reflects all light away like a mirror, appearing pitch black, and then bursts into brilliant white diamond fire when tilted toward a light source.

12. Perlage Circular Graining and Micro-Dust Trapping Science

Hidden surfaces on mainplates and dial-side plates are decorated with Perlage (circular graining). Using a rotating wooden peg coated with abrasive emery paste, the artisan applies overlapping circular stipples. Beyond its shimmering iridescent beauty, perlage serves an essential functional purpose: the microscopic concentric grain grooves act as reservoirs that trap ambient micro-dust particles, preventing them from migrating into delicate balance pivots.
